# ASDLC Meta-Specs: repository audit and research assessment

**Audit date:** 18 November 2026  
**Repository:** [jvanheerikhuize/asdlc-meta-specs][repo]  
**Revision:** [`22f6fc87097d7dd5814cb42d5d934f7fe51a7196`][revision], `main`, committed 18 November 2026 at 14:24:50 UTC  
**Purpose:** Assess the theory, evidence, corpus, implementation, and practical direction for spec-driven development.  
**Change scope:** Report only. No repository files, branches, settings, or workflows were changed.

## 1. Executive judgment

**Yes: this is a worthwhile direction for spec-driven development, if you position it as a lightweight evidence, rationale, and uncertainty layer around normal engineering.**

**No: the present evidence does not justify claims that the corpus is complete, one acceptance scenario is sufficient, stack migration requires no specification changes, or the system can reliably regenerate equivalent software.**

The strongest idea is making the *grounds of a requirement* explicit and revisitable. Facts, assumptions, and unanswered questions should not look identical to an implementing agent. Connecting those grounds to requirements, acceptance examples, and downstream change impact is useful engineering.

The weakest part is that the repository sometimes treats a plausible idea, an anecdote, or the existence of an implementation as a verified general fact. Its checker then propagates those declarations into green readiness indicators. That creates a dangerous mismatch with the project's promise of epistemic honesty.

**The central problem is not insufficient structure. It is that structural consistency is being presented as stronger assurance than the underlying evidence earns.**

The most actionable technical findings are date serialization that can break publication, acceptance text accepted without scenario validation, exports that drop epistemic context, and validation errors that are not handled fail-closed at publication and display boundaries.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Conceptual foundation | Substantial support from requirements engineering, traceability, provenance, and assurance-case work. |
| Distinctive contribution | A practical combination and developer experience, not a new foundational theory of requirements. |
| Evidence for this particular method | Early and weak. Useful dogfooding is recorded, but comparative benefit and generalization are not established. |
| Implementation | A credible small prototype with working CI and a usable public workspace, plus important correctness and assurance gaps. |
| Production assurance | Not sufficient as a standalone readiness, correctness, or autonomous-delivery gate. |
| Recommended investment | Continue, narrow the claims, repair the trust boundaries, and run independent evaluations before expanding the framework. |

My recommendation is **spec-anchored development with explicit uncertainty**, not unrestricted "specs replace engineering" or "regeneration proves equivalence."

## 3. What is genuinely good

### Explicit uncertainty is better than silently invented certainty

The distinction between an assumption with a candidate answer and a question with no candidate answer is useful. A question is not simply an assumption with low confidence. Keeping those separate makes investigation and escalation more actionable.

### `because` links preserve information that ordinary tickets often lose

A requirement without its rationale is difficult to revisit intelligently. Your model preserves the connection between an intended behavior and the domain knowledge or belief used to justify it. This is especially valuable when that knowledge changes.

### Change-impact analysis is a practical use of the graph

The graph is not merely decorative. Following downstream links and presenting specifications in dependency order can help contributors identify work that needs review. This is a credible application of traceability, although the graph can only find dependencies that someone actually recorded.

### The implementation is small enough to understand

Plain-text records, version control, one main Python module, and a zero-build web interface make the approach inspectable and portable. That is a meaningful advantage over a large proprietary requirements platform.

### The workspace makes the model tangible

The live dashboard and graph worked during the audit. Graph selection showed a feature's own grounds and dependency boundaries. The source includes keyboard-operable graph buttons, focus handling, labels, and reduced-motion handling. These are positive implementation details, although they are not a completed accessibility assessment.

### Separating behavior from incidental implementation choices is sensible

Keeping a framework choice out of a business rule can reduce unnecessary specification churn. The external technical binding is a reasonable mechanism. The caveat is that essential interface constraints and technology-dependent semantics must not disappear in the process.

### The roadmap resists premature metamodel expansion

The stated policy of adding concepts only when observed usage justifies them is good. Keep that discipline. The next improvements should make the existing model more trustworthy, not multiply record types.

## 4. How the theories stand up to research

| Theory or mechanism | Research grounding | What the evidence does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements depend on knowledge and assumptions about the surrounding world | Zave and Jackson distinguish domain knowledge, requirements, and specifications; Doyle's truth-maintenance work explains dependency-based belief revision. [S1] [S2] | A `because` link is not a logical proof, and a status label is not a calibrated measure of certainty. |
| Preserving rationale and origin matters | Gotel and Finkelstein distinguish pre-requirements and post-requirements traceability; NASA guidance emphasizes stakeholder needs, rationale, decisions, and traceability. [S3] [S4] | Version history alone does not capture stakeholder intent or guarantee meaningful review. |
| Clarifying ambiguity before code generation can improve results | ClarifyGPT provides direct experimental evidence on benchmark code-generation tasks. [S5] | It does not show that one Given/When/Then example is sufficient, or that clarification should be eliminated. |
| Requirements problems are important in practice | NaPiRE studies practitioners' reported problems, causes, and effects across 228 companies in ten countries. [S6] | A survey of reported problems does not prove that this particular five-record method prevents project failure. |
| Evidence should carry provenance | W3C PROV models derivation, responsible agents, activities, and time. [S7] | A nonempty evidence sentence does not establish that the source is reliable or supports the claim. |
| Claims should be connected to evidence through explicit reasoning | OMG SACM defines assurance cases in terms of auditable claims, arguments, and evidence. [S8] | Collecting links without the argument that makes them relevant is not equivalent to an assurance case. |
| Precise specifications can improve design assurance | AWS describes useful industrial experience with formal specification and model checking. [S9] | Those results cannot be transferred wholesale to natural-language records plus a metadata checker. |
| Examples and current tests may miss incorrect behavior | EvalPlus strengthens code-generation benchmarks with substantially expanded tests. [S10] | Passing a small example suite, or citing a requirement in a comment, does not establish correctness. |
| Agent context should be selected, not merely accumulated | Lost in the Middle shows position-sensitive use of long contexts in the models and retrieval tasks studied. [S11] | It does not prove that every current model has the same limitation, or that atomic files alone solve context selection. |
| AI-development productivity must be measured end to end | METR's experiments and follow-up reports show the importance of task selection, model vintage, actual time, and measurement bias; DORA emphasizes the surrounding organizational system. [S12] [S13] [S14] [S15] | Self-reported speed, absence of questions, or generated artifact volume is not a reliable substitute for delivered value. |
| SDD benefits from distinguishing intent, design, and implementation | Spec Kit, OpenSpec, and Kiro all provide structured workflows, while Bockeler distinguishes spec-first, spec-anchored, and spec-as-source approaches. [S16] [S17] [S18] [S19] | Tool documentation and practitioner opinion are not independent experimental proof that SDD, or your implementation, is superior. |

### 4.1 Your strongest empirical citation is useful, but narrower than the surrounding claims

The ClarifyGPT publication abstract and authors' replication package report GPT-4 Pass@1 improving from **70.96% to 80.80% on MBPP-sanitized**, with a human evaluation involving ten participants. That is a **9.84 percentage-point improvement** in that evaluation. [S5]

It supports:

> Detecting ambiguity and obtaining useful clarification can improve generated solutions in some settings.

It does not support:

> One acceptance scenario per requirement removes the need for clarification in general.

Those are different hypotheses. In fact, a system that never asks questions might be confidently inventing missing requirements. Successful clarification is often a desirable behavior.

### 4.2 The intellectual ancestry is a strength, not a reason to dismiss the project

Your work resembles an approachable combination of:

- Requirements rationale and pre-requirements traceability.
- Dependency-directed belief revision.
- Claims, arguments, and evidence.
- Behavior examples and test traceability.
- Plain-text, version-controlled engineering artifacts.

That means the underlying instincts are grounded. It also means the strongest novelty claim is **a lightweight, usable integration for agent-assisted development**, not a newly discovered axiom that requirements can be derived from facts alone.

### 4.3 Counterevidence and cautions should remain visible

AWS's formal-methods experience supports precise modeling, including failures and concurrency. The same article explicitly discusses limits, including performance phenomena its models did not adequately capture. Your informal checker offers substantially less semantic assurance. [S9]

Bockeler's comparative SDD analysis raises concerns about review overload, maintenance, abstraction mismatch, and combining model-driven-development rigidity with LLM nondeterminism. This is informed practitioner analysis, not a controlled experiment, but it is directly relevant to your roadmap. [S19]

METR's early-2025 study found a 19% slowdown for its particular experienced-developer sample and tools. **Do not present that as a current universal result.** The original post now marks that result as out of date, and its February 2026 update explains serious selection and measurement problems in the follow-up. Its May 2026 survey reports perceived gains while explicitly warning that self-reports can be wrong. [S12] [S13] [S14]

The lesson is not "AI is slow" or "SDD is proven." It is: **measure the complete workflow, and be as careful with your own positive findings as you are with negative studies.**

## 6. Conceptual corrections before adding more features

### 6.1 Facts constrain requirements; they do not supply all the reasons for wanting them

A fact such as "a checker runs quickly" does not by itself imply "every requirement must cite grounds" or "specifications must have these tiers."

Requirements also express stakeholder goals, policy, legal obligations, risk tolerances, and deliberate choices. NASA's design guidance explicitly starts from stakeholder expectations, objectives, constraints, and decision authority, iterating between these and design. [S4]

The current [REQ-0002][req2] and [REQ-0003][req3] both cite the checker-performance fact as their grounds. That is at most an enabling consideration, not an adequate explanation for their substantive policies.

Keep the compact model if you wish. You do not immediately need a sixth record type. You do need to distinguish:

| Kind of justification | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Observed condition | A measured workload has particular characteristics. |
| Assumption | An external system will continue to satisfy a condition. |
| Authoritative obligation | A product owner or applicable policy requires an outcome. |
| Design decision | The team chose one tradeoff over alternatives. |
| Argument | Given the goals and conditions, this requirement is an appropriate response. |

A requirement's rationale or authority field can carry this distinction without pretending that preferences are empirical facts.

A fact can legitimately record that an authorized stakeholder approved a policy. Be explicit that the observation is the approval and its scope, rather than suggesting that the policy preference itself was experimentally proven.

### 6.2 "Active" should not mean "known true and ready to ship"

The checker verifies declared relationships and selected structural rules. It does not independently establish whether evidence is credible, relevant, representative, current, or correctly interpreted.

Use language such as **accepted for the stated scope**, **declared grounds consistent**, or **grounding coverage**. Reserve implementation verification, operational readiness, and stakeholder acceptance for separate evidence.

The dashboard's current "proven," "known," and "every spec is ready" wording is stronger than its inputs warrant.

### 6.3 A list of grounds hides important differences in support

The current model effectively treats invalidation of any cited ground as requiring a broken requirement. That can be a useful conservative workflow rule, but it is not a general law.

Some grounds are necessary assumptions; others are background context, alternative justifications, or independent corroboration. Losing one supporting observation does not always invalidate the requirement. Conversely, several individually true facts may still fail to justify a requirement because the argument is wrong.

Initially, document the intended meaning of `because` and require human review of invalidation. Avoid claiming a general certainty calculus.

### 6.4 Blocking uncertainty must not block learning

Blocking a high-risk production change on an unresolved authorization rule is sensible. Blocking a reversible prototype needed to test an assumption can be self-defeating.

Distinguish permission to investigate from permission to release. Allow explicitly scoped experiments with unresolved assumptions while retaining production gates. Otherwise the workflow can create an incentive to manufacture facts merely to turn everything green.

### 6.5 File atomicity is not behavioral atomicity

One file per record helps editing, but several records contain many independently testable obligations. REQ-0005 covers multiple renderers, filtering, stack binding, metadata, and open-question presentation. REQ-0012 combines browsing, detail views, capture, companion edits, and themes.

Do not split every sentence into a file. Split concerns when they change independently, have different rationale, or need separately tracked acceptance evidence.

### 6.6 Clean-room reconstruction is a good experiment, not a completeness theorem

[PR 17][pr17] reports an isolated reconstruction and byte-identical rendered specification files. That is useful evidence of recoverability for a particular exercise.

It does not establish full application equivalence. Byte-identical renderer output does not test capture failure handling, graph interactions, data validation, every CLI diagnostic, or unspecified edge cases. A reconstructor also writing its own tests can reproduce the same omissions.

Keep the experiment, but require held-out tests and independent review. Describe it as a dated, bounded reconstruction result rather than proof that the repository is completely specified.

## 7. Implementation and assurance findings

**Priority meanings:** P1 should be addressed before treating the framework as a trustworthy delivery gate. P2 should be addressed before broader adoption or stronger scale and portability claims. These are engineering priorities, not vulnerability ratings.

### A01 - P1: Evidence declarations can launder weak claims into green assurance

**Basis:** Current corpus and [checker implementation][check].

The fact check principally requires a truthy `evidence` value. It does not evaluate whether evidence supports the statement. This limitation is normal for a structural checker; the problem is the stronger product language and the current fact promotions.

**Recommendation:** Establish a human-reviewable evidence standard, narrow or reopen the overclaimed facts, and describe the checker as enforcing declared consistency. Do not add a second LLM that automatically certifies the first LLM's evidence as truth.

### A02 - P1: The model lacks an explicit place for normative intent and the argument from evidence

**Basis:** Current `because` usage and the conceptual issues in section 6.

Some current links explain implementation feasibility rather than why the behavior is required. This makes the graph look more grounded than its actual reasoning.

**Recommendation:** Record stakeholder authority and rationale alongside empirical grounds. A compact additional section or field is enough initially.

### A03 - P1: Exported artifacts do not consistently preserve the epistemic guardrails

**Basis:** [Markdown][render-md], [Gherkin][render-gh], [OpenSpec][render-os], and [Spec Kit][render-sk] renderer source. This is directly observable in the functions.

| Information | Markdown | Gherkin | OpenSpec | Spec Kit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Requirement statements | Yes | Not as separate statements | Yes | Yes |
| Requirement status | Yes | Tags for non-active requirements | Not emitted | Yes |
| Grounds | IDs, statuses, titles | Not emitted | Not emitted | IDs |
| Full supporting fact and evidence contents | No | No | No | No |
| Spec-level open questions | Yes | Not emitted | Yes | Yes |
| Requirement-level open questions | Yes | Question-ID tags | Yes | Yes |

The [feature handoff workflow][readme] can give an agent only the rendered spec and technical binding. Such an agent does not receive the full evidence and assumption-validation context.

An especially important source-derived case is a question blocking a specification rather than an individual requirement: the Gherkin output does not carry that spec-level block.

**Recommendation:** Define a preservation contract for every format. Keep each output honest about omissions, carry release-blocking uncertainty in every agent-consumable packet, and include the relevant supporting subgraph or an explicit source manifest. Do not describe partial projections as lossless.

### A04 - P1: Acceptance text is not actually checked as Given/When/Then

**Basis:** [`Record.scenarios()`][record] and the active-requirement check.

The parser splits the acceptance section into nonempty blocks. It does not verify that those blocks contain meaningful Given, When, and Then steps in the correct structure.

A section containing only `TBD` therefore satisfies the nonempty-scenario predicate in the inspected source. This is a source-derived counterexample, not a newly executed repository test.

**Recommendation:** Specify and validate the supported scenario grammar. Add a focused malformed-scenario case. If standard Gherkin compatibility is promised, validate generated files with an actual supported parser rather than only counting scenario labels.

### A05 - P1: Traceability verifies text citations, not tests or implementation correctness

**Basis:** [`verify_traceability()`][verify], [REQ-0013][req13], and the [test suite][tests].

The verifier scans text for requirement references. A comment can satisfy it. A bare requirement citation intentionally exempts that requirement's individual scenarios from specific citation warnings. Unknown requirement IDs are not rejected in the same way as an out-of-range scenario on a known requirement.

This behavior is substantially documented in REQ-0013, so the bare-citation shortcut is **not an accidental implementation deviation**. The mismatch is with the stronger impression created by "verify" and "closing the loop."

The three workspace-contract tests inspect source strings. They do not execute the browser behavior described by the workspace requirements.

**Recommendation:** Call the current mechanism a citation check. Keep it as a useful lint, but separately connect stable acceptance IDs to executed results, commit and corpus revisions, and meaningful assertions. Add a few real interaction checks for the highest-risk workspace flows.

### A06 - P1: A valid unquoted fact-review date can break index publication

**Basis:** Record loading, [`to_index()`][index], JSON output in [`main()`][main], and the independent library probe.

The contract says parsed dates are converted to ISO strings before JSON serialization. The implementation copies metadata directly into the index and passes the result to ordinary `json.dumps` without date conversion.

An unquoted `review_after: 2030-01-01` is parsed by PyYAML as a date object. The independent probe produced:

```text
2030-01-01 loads as date
JSON conversion error: Object of type date is not JSON serializable
```

The quoted equivalent loads as a string and serializes.

No current fact has a review date, which helps explain why the current corpus and CI do not expose this path. The probe verifies the library behavior; the repository failure path is established by source inspection, not by running its CLI.

**Recommendation:** Normalize date-valued metadata at the index boundary and test index serialization with an ordinary unquoted review date. Update the corpus contract before implementation.

### A07 - P1: Publication is not gated by validation, and global errors can disappear from the UI

**Basis:** [Pages workflow][pages], [check workflow][workflow], [`to_index()`][index], [`main()`][main], and [dashboard/task logic][app].

The Pages workflow independently generates and deploys an index. It does not depend on the check workflow succeeding. Ordinary validation errors are included in index summary data, but index generation still returns success.

There is a second problem: issues are attached to records by interpreting the beginning of an error string as a record ID. A global dependency-cycle error has no record filename. It is therefore not attached to any record's issue list. The frontend does not use the global summary error count to guard its reassuring dashboard callouts.

A source-derived failure path is:

1. A dependency cycle is introduced while record statuses remain active.
2. The checker reports a global error.
3. Index generation includes an error count but no corresponding per-record issue.
4. The dashboard can derive no corrective tasks and still present reassuring readiness text.

This is not a claim that the current deployed graph contains a cycle, or that branch protections currently allow such a change. Those controls were not established in this audit.

**Recommendation:** Gate deployment on successful validation and tests. Preserve global diagnostics as first-class index fields. Make the UI visibly invalid whenever the source snapshot has validation errors.

### A08 - P2: The record contract is only partially enforced

**Basis:** [Loader and record methods][record], [checker][check], and [technical contract][stack].

The implementation does not comprehensively validate front-matter types, required body sections, nonblank titles, assumption validation plans, or confidence ranges. Relationship validation is uneven.

For example, `depends_on` is checked for unknown targets and the skeleton/feature restriction, but not generally required to target an SPC. Promotion validation also does not fully enforce target types and reciprocal relationships in every direction.

Malformed field shapes can trigger ordinary language exceptions instead of a clear record-specific diagnostic.

**Recommendation:** Add small, explicit type and relationship guards before graph traversal. This does not require a large schema framework. Publish a machine-readable schema when interoperability needs it, but do not defer basic validation until then.

### A09 - P2: Rounded readiness is used as a correctness gate

**Basis:** [`readiness()`][readiness] and the active-spec check.

The gate compares a rounded percentage with 100 rather than checking that every requirement is active. With 199 active requirements out of 200, the calculation returns 100. That can admit a spec containing an inactive requirement.

The current 31-record corpus does not hit this case. It is a scale-dependent, source-derived defect whose numerical behavior was independently checked.

Readiness also does not recursively account for dependency readiness. A feature can have all its own requirements active while depending on a draft or blocked prerequisite.

**Recommendation:** Use exact predicates for gates, percentages only for display. Decide whether the metric means "own requirements grounded" or "entire dependency closure buildable," and name it accordingly.

### A10 - P2: Scenario IDs are positional, so citations can silently change meaning

**Basis:** Scenario enumeration in the renderers and the citation verifier.

Inserting or reordering acceptance blocks changes the meaning of identifiers such as `REQ-0005#2`. An old citation can remain syntactically valid while now referring to a different behavior.

**Recommendation:** Use stable explicit acceptance IDs, or version and fingerprint scenario content so that changed meaning requires review. This matters more than adding more citation counts.

### A11 - P2: Freshness and snapshot provenance are incomplete

**Basis:** Fact metadata, [index shape][index], and the frontend's `tone`, `effective`, and `stateRow` functions.

The published index contains repository identity, summary, spec order, and records, but no corpus commit, generation timestamp, or schema version. Consumers cannot confidently tie a displayed result to a source revision.

The backend marks a fact stale only after its review date. The browser's timestamp comparison can consider the date stale during that date. More importantly, segmented state bars group facts using their declared status rather than consistently using the stale-fact tone.

A static deployment also cannot regenerate checker diagnostics as calendar time passes unless a rebuild or another explicit mechanism does so.

**Recommendation:** Include revision and generation metadata, define one date policy, and make all status surfaces use the same semantics. Assign review dates selectively to time-sensitive observations instead of treating all facts as expiring identically.

### A12 - P2: Broader adoption needs a few operational contracts, not more grand theory

**Basis:** Repository inventory, [capture/startup source][app], workflows, and [dependency declaration][dependencies].

Important adoption gaps include:

- No license file or GitHub-recognized license at the audited revision. Public visibility alone does not grant a general reuse license.
- Sequential IDs allocated from the loaded snapshot, so concurrent proposals can choose the same next ID.
- Local draft-storage errors are swallowed, without informing the user that persistence failed.
- Startup handles network and JSON-parse errors but not a general malformed-index schema after loading.
- No defined browser/accessibility conformance target or automated interaction coverage.
- A floating `PyYAML>=6` dependency and only one CI interpreter version, despite a broader documented runtime range.

These are prototype limitations, not evidence that the whole direction is wrong. The dependency declaration is not by itself evidence of a security vulnerability.

**Recommendation:** Choose a license, specify snapshot and draft failure behavior, test the documented runtime range, and cover the critical user flows before recommending the tool as a reusable team platform.

The current suite is valuable. The question is whether its evidence matches each requirement's breadth.

| REQ-0009: machine-readable index | Core shape and selected fields are covered. Date normalization, global diagnostics, and source provenance need work. |
| REQ-0010: dashboard and task queue | One source-text test checks labels and a date-related token, not actual prioritization, stale-state display, or invalid-snapshot behavior. |
| REQ-0011: lineage graph | One source-text test checks implementation fragments. The selected live graph worked, but automated traversal and interaction assertions are absent. |
| REQ-0012: browser, capture, theme | Source strings are checked. Companion-edit correctness, stale snapshots, persistence failures, and interaction behavior are not behaviorally verified. |
| REQ-0013: acceptance traceability | Citation scanning has focused tests, but it is not a mapping to executed assertions. Positional-ID drift and unknown IDs remain concerns. |

The three active specs are therefore better described as **fully active according to the current declaration rules**, not fully verified implementations.

## 9. Positioning relative to current SDD tools

| Approach | Main emphasis in the material reviewed | Your best relationship to it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub Spec Kit | Structured intent, clarification, planning, tasks, and associated artifacts. Its current template also calls for measurable success criteria. [S16] | Supply grounded intent and unresolved questions, then let its workflow handle planning and delivery. A similar-looking Markdown document is not the whole integration. |
| OpenSpec | Changes, proposals, behavior specifications, design, tasks, and evolving project specifications. [S17] | Provide rationale and uncertainty provenance that survives the change lifecycle. Validate against an explicit supported version and workflow. |
| Kiro | Requirements, design, tasks, review, and optional property-based correctness checks. [S18] | Complement its implementation feedback with scope-aware evidence and assumptions. Do not imply that examples alone provide the same assurance as stronger properties. |
| ASDLC Meta-Specs | Explicit grounds, questions, graph impact, compact records, multiple projections, and a triage workspace. | Be a trustworthy, small knowledge and assurance layer rather than rebuilding every downstream tool's lifecycle. |

The [current Spec Kit template][speckit-template] contains information not represented as a first-class concept in your corpus, such as measurable success criteria, prioritized user stories, and independent tests.

This does not make your renderer useless or necessarily syntactically invalid. It means that a universal "no additional fields needed" claim needs much stronger evidence.

The same applies to architectural decision records: alternatives, consequences, and decision authority are not automatically recoverable from a list of facts and behavioral requirements.

**Recommended positioning:**

> A small, version-controlled record graph that makes requirements' rationale, assumptions, and unresolved questions explicit, and carries that context into agent-assisted implementation and review.

That claim is useful, differentiated, and much easier to substantiate than "a complete universal source model for all spec-driven tools."

## 10. The smallest worthwhile evolution of the model

Preserve the five record types for now. Strengthen their meaning before expanding the ontology.

| Area | Minimal improvement |
| --- | --- |
| Facts | State the observation's scope, source or artifact, observation date, and limitations; identify who accepts it. |
| Assumptions | Require a falsifiable validation plan, outcome criterion, and clear statement of where the assumption is relied upon. |
| Questions | Distinguish questions answerable by research from choices requiring stakeholder authority. |
| Requirements | Preserve intent, rationale, and authority as well as empirical grounds. |
| Acceptance | Use stable identities and observable outcomes; include negative and boundary behavior where relevant. |
| Specs | Define whether readiness concerns local grounds, dependencies, implementation verification, or release approval. Do not compress these into one green percentage. |
| Exports | Publish a field-preservation contract and identify the source revision. |
| Evidence review | Make promotion a reviewable judgment, not a label change inferred from successful code generation. |

Not every observation needs a large template. A short scoped evidence note can be enough. Expensive claims, high-impact assumptions, and generalizations need stronger support.

Likewise, stack neutrality should exclude **incidental implementation choices**, not essential requirements. A mandated interface standard, data format, deployment restriction, or external-system behavior may belong in the behavioral contract even if it mentions a technology.

The current finite keyword guard should remain a convenience lint with documented exceptions, not the definition of good abstraction.

## 11. Prioritized action plan

### First: repair epistemic honesty in the corpus

Before changing implementation behavior:

- Correct FCT-0007's contradicted renderer evidence.
- Narrow FCT-0001, FCT-0004, FCT-0008, and FCT-0009 to what is actually supported.
- Reopen the broad sufficiency and migration hypotheses behind FCT-0005 and FCT-0006 unless reproducible evidence can be supplied.
- Record the resulting changes to downstream grounds and status without treating the audit's judgment as an experimental result.
- Replace completeness and "proven" claims with scoped, accurately described observations.
- Add the missing rationale and authority for policies currently justified by unrelated performance observations.

**Exit condition:** A reviewer can follow each important claim to evidence and see its limitations without having to trust the author's or agent's confidence.

### Second: make the checker and publication boundary trustworthy

Update the relevant corpus requirements first, then implement focused corrections:

- Date normalization through index generation.
- Real scenario-shape validation.
- Exact activation predicates and typed dependency edges.
- Global diagnostic preservation and a fail-closed invalid-snapshot UI.
- Validation-gated publication.
- Accurate naming of citation checks and stable acceptance identities.
- Explicit preservation of blocking uncertainty in exported agent context.

**Exit condition:** Valid documented inputs work, invalid states cannot be presented as healthy, and an export cannot silently erase a release-blocking question.

### Third: validate value before expanding scope

Run a small comparative pilot with held-out correctness checks. Measure authoring, review, clarification, maintenance, and rework as well as implementation time.

**Exit condition:** There is evidence that the extra structure improves outcomes or reduces total effort in a stated setting, rather than only producing more attractive artifacts.

### Only then: add capabilities justified by observed friction

Potential additions include richer evidence records, reusable constraints, cross-repository identifiers, or more automated capture. None is the immediate bottleneck.

Do not build a larger agent orchestration system to compensate for an unvalidated evidence model.

## 12. A research plan that could genuinely validate the direction

### 12.1 Separate the hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Suitable measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Explicit grounds reduce unsupported implementation choices | Independently reviewed rate of behavior not justified by the task, requirements, or approved decisions. |
| Explicit questions improve ambiguity handling | Appropriate questions asked, unresolved ambiguities detected, and incorrect silent assumptions avoided. |
| The graph improves maintenance | Time and missed dependencies when a ground changes, including deliberate stale or contradictory inputs. |
| The workflow saves effort | Total human time and total elapsed delivery time, including corpus authoring, review, repairs, and synchronization. |
| A corpus supports reconstruction | Held-out behavioral and integration checks on independently rebuilt implementations. |
| Stack separation improves portability | Actual implementations on materially different stacks, contract differences, record edits, and migration effort. |
| Atomic records reduce conflict costs | Concurrent edit trials, textual conflicts, ID collisions, semantic conflicts, and resolution time. |
| Renderers preserve intended meaning | Field-preservation checks, consumer validation, and correct handling of uncertainty in the target workflow. |

Do not collapse these into "agent completed the task" or "no questions were asked."

### 12.2 Use a strong baseline

A reasonable pilot compares:

| Condition | Inputs |
| --- | --- |
| A: good existing practice | Clear task description, ordinary repository context, human-reviewed requirements, and existing tests. |
| B: ordinary spec-first | Comparable information in a conventional maintained specification and acceptance examples, without the epistemic graph. |
| C: meta-spec workflow | The same underlying information, explicit grounds/questions, graph checks, and generated implementation context. |

The baseline must not be a deliberately vague one-line prompt. Otherwise you measure the benefit of giving an agent more useful information, not the benefit of the record model.

For an information-controlled comparison, equalize substantive content across conditions. For a practical adoption comparison, also count the effort needed to create and maintain that content.

### 12.3 Control the obvious confounders

Keep model version, harness, tools, task budget, and human-intervention rules comparable. Randomize or counterbalance task assignment. Include greenfield work, brownfield changes, error paths, and changed assumptions.

Use fresh implementation contexts so earlier solutions do not leak into later conditions. Freeze the corpus and technical binding for reconstruction trials. Record deviations rather than silently helping one condition more than another.

A modest batch of matched tasks can establish feasibility and expose large problems. It cannot support universal claims. Use observed variability and a chosen minimum meaningful effect to determine the size of a larger study.

### 12.4 Make correctness independent of the generating agent

Use held-out tests and a reviewer who did not write the implementation. Evaluate behavior outside the supplied happy-path examples.

For the meta-spec tool itself, useful cases include malformed front matter, scenario insertion, non-SPC dependencies, cycles, unquoted dates, broken index loads, draft-storage failure, stale snapshots, and conflicting proposals.

Where relevant, add properties such as "invalid source data is never displayed as healthy." Property-based tests provide broader evidence than a few examples, but they still are not mathematical proof. Kiro's own documentation makes that limitation explicit. [S18]

### 12.5 Predeclare the decision rule

Choose practical success criteria before looking at results. Examples include:

- No increase in severe behavioral defects or unauthorized assumptions.
- No loss of required clarification or stakeholder control.
- A meaningful reduction in total human effort, or a clearly valued increase in correctness and auditability at an accepted cost.
- Better change-impact recall without an unmanageable false-positive workload.
- Benefits persisting through later changes, not just the first implementation.

Do not invent a universal percentage target after seeing a favorable run. Publish negative cases and the maintenance costs.

### 12.6 Preserve enough artifacts to make promotion reviewable

For a significant result, retain the exact claim, source revision, task set, prompts, model/tool versions, configuration, evaluation procedure, raw outcomes, interventions, and limitations.

A validated observation should read like:

> In this dated evaluation, with these tasks and tools, this result was observed under these conditions.

The broader claim should remain a hypothesis until evidence supports generalization.

## 13. Where this approach fits, and where it does not

**Likely good fit:** Long-lived software with changing domain assumptions; work handed between people and agents; maintenance where rationale is often lost; projects where traceability and review are worth their cost.

It can contribute to assurance-heavy environments, but the current prototype is not a substitute for their required verification, governance, or compliance processes.

**Use selectively:** Small projects with clear boundaries, where a few important assumptions and questions can be tracked without documenting every trivial fact.

**Poor fit if imposed rigidly:** Exploratory product discovery, disposable prototypes, or rapidly changing ideas where forcing every requirement to rest only on "facts" prevents the experiments needed to learn.

**Not currently justified:** Treating the corpus as a complete substitute for implementation inspection, independent tests, architecture analysis, operational feedback, or human authorization.

## 14. Research and citation hygiene

Several repository references are directionally useful but need more precise treatment:

| Current usage | Needed correction |
| --- | --- |
| ClarifyGPT's numeric improvement | Keep the exact benchmark, model, participant context, and Pass@1 metric. It is not a result about your framework. |
| NaPiRE as a failure argument | Describe it as an empirical survey of reported requirements problems, not a causal demonstration that your method prevents failures. |
| Lutz labeled 1993 | The linked NASA record identifies a publication date of 1 January 1994. This may concern a different version, but the citation should identify the exact work/version. |
| Zave/Jackson and software evolution used to justify review dates | Distinguish foundational ideas about domain knowledge and change from the design decision to require or recommend a calendar control. |
| Dogfooding and clean-room results | Link dated artifacts, protocol, and scope. A PR summary is evidence that a result was reported, not an independent replication. |
| Vendor/tool documentation | Use it to establish supported conventions and workflows, not as neutral proof of productivity or correctness. |

The NASA metadata discrepancy is verified at the [linked NTRS record][lutz]. I did not inspect that paper's full text and do not infer its detailed findings from the repository's description.

## 15. Final recommendation

**Keep going, but make the project less certain and more testable.**

There is a strong engineering idea here: retain the reasons for requirements, distinguish unknowns from beliefs, and make the consequences of changing those grounds visible to humans and agents.

There is not yet a strong empirical case for the universal claims wrapped around it. The current all-green corpus is not proof that uncertainty has been removed; in several places it reflects assumptions promoted beyond the evidence.

The most credible future is:

> Evidence-aware, spec-anchored development with explicit intent, bounded claims, maintained rationale, and independently checked behavior.

The least credible future is:

> A universal specification graph whose green status is treated as proof that an agent can reconstruct any equivalent implementation.

**The next concrete task should be an evidence-correction pass on the nine facts, followed by the small trust-boundary fixes and an independently evaluated maintenance pilot.** That would do more to establish this as a good direction than adding another record type, renderer, or automation.
